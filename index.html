For grown-ups: the Chinese methods

Children in Chinese Year 1 classrooms build mental maths on a few clear ideas. Each game here practises one of them. Tap the green speaker to hear any question read aloud.

  • 分与合 Split and join. Every number up to 10 is two smaller numbers (7 is 3 and 4). Children drill these until they are automatic, often drawn as a "number bond" tree.
  • 凑十 Ten friends. The pairs that make 10: 1 and 9, 2 and 8, and so on. These are the backbone of everything that follows.
  • 凑十法 Make ten. To work out 8 + 5, take 2 from the 5 to fill the 8 up to 10, then add what is left: 10 + 3 = 13. This replaces counting on one by one.
  • 算盘 Abacus. A top bead is worth 5 and each bottom bead is worth 1, so 7 is seen as 5 and 2. The traditional suanpan has 2 top and 5 bottom beads per rod; this game uses the simpler 1 and 4 school abacus.

How tricky? Easy uses small numbers and lots of dots to count. Medium covers numbers to 10 and adding to 18. Hard takes away the counting help, uses teen numbers, and makes children work out each step themselves. The level applies to every game and can be changed at any time.
